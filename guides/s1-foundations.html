How the S-1 Makes a Sound

Roland AIRA Compact S-1 + MiniLab 3·Plain-language course with pictures·2026-10-28 17:40 PDT

Welcome. Start here.

This course teaches you how the S-1 makes a sound. You do not need to know anything first. Each lesson is short. Each lesson builds on the one before it.

How every lesson works

  • Goal says what you will be able to do at the end.
  • Remember gives you the two or three ideas from last time.
  • Set your S-1 like this lists every knob and setting. Copy it. Do not skip it.
  • The big idea is the one new thing in this lesson.
  • Try it tells you what to do, one step at a time.
  • If it sounds wrong lists the common slip-ups and the fix.
  • Check yourself is a tiny quiz. Tick the boxes when you can do each thing.
  • Save it tells you where to save. Then a button takes you to the next lesson.
Two rules for the whole course

1. Turn your headphones down before you start. Some sounds get loud fast.

2. Change one thing at a time. Listen. Then change the next thing.

Words we use for knobs

We say knob positions like a clock. 7 o'clock is all the way left. That is the lowest setting. We also call it MIN. 5 o'clock is all the way right. That is the highest setting. We call it MAX. 12 o'clock is straight up, the middle.

Knob names like Knob 1 or Fader 2 are the MiniLab 3 controls. The MiniLab must be on its "Roland S1 v2" program. The full map is in Lesson 11. You can do every lesson on the S-1 alone too.

00 · Housekeeping: slots, saving, and the clean start

GOAL

You can pick an empty slot, wipe it clean, set the knobs to the clean start, save it, and never break a factory sound.

On the S-1 a sound and its notes live together. Roland calls that pair a pattern. Think of it as a slot.

Where the 64 slots live

There are 4 banks. Each bank has 16 slots. That is 64 slots. We write a slot as bank-number. So 2-05 means bank 2, slot 5.

BankWhat is in it from the factoryWhat we use it for in this course
1 (1-01 to 1-16)Roland's 16 factory sounds. They can be overwritten. There is no lock.Listen only. Never save here.
2 (2-01 to 2-16)EmptyLesson slots. Lesson 03 saves to 2-03, Lesson 04 to 2-04, and so on.
3 (3-01 to 3-16)EmptyYour own sounds. Save the ones you like here.
4 (4-01 to 4-16)EmptyScratch. Mess around here. It does not matter if you wreck it. Slot 4-15 is the one keeper: the clean start.
The one way to lose a factory sound

You are in bank 1, you change the sound, and you press SHIFT + pad 16 (WRITE). That saves over Roland's sound. So: always check the display shows a 2, 3 or 4 first before you save.

If you do wreck one, the fix is a factory reset. Hold the HOLD pad while you turn the power on. The display shows FACt (factory reset). Press the blinking D-MOTION button. Wait for donE (done). Turn the S-1 off and on. This wipes all 64 slots, so back up first (see below).

Step 1 · Pick a slot

  1. Hold the PATTERN button. The pads for the 4 banks light up.
  2. Still holding PATTERN, press pad 1, 2, 3 or 4 to pick the bank.
  3. Let go of PATTERN.
  4. Press a pad 1 to 16 to pick the slot number.

The display shows the slot, like 2.03 (bank 2, slot 3). If a sound is playing, the new slot waits until the old one finishes.

Step 2 · Wipe the slot clean (this is "init")

"Init" is short for initialize. It means: erase this slot. The notes go away. The sound goes back to Roland's blank sound. Every hidden SHIFT setting goes back to normal too.

  1. Hold SHIFT. Press pad 15 (MENU).
  2. Turn the TEMPO/VALUE knob until the display shows init (initialize).
  3. Press pad 2 (ENTER).
  4. Press pad 1 (EXIT) to leave the menu.

Init is not saved by itself. If you turn the S-1 off now, the old slot comes back. That is fine. We save at the end of each lesson.

Step 3 · The clean start (knobs match the sound)

The S-1 has a catch. The knobs can point one way while the sound is set another way. That happens every time you change slot. To make the sound match the knobs, hold SHIFT and press HOLD (MANUAL). We call this the clean start. Set the knobs like the picture first. Then press SHIFT + HOLD.

Step 4 · Play it

Press any keyboard pad. The 13 big pads at the bottom are a one-octave keyboard. You should hear one plain, buzzy note. That is the clean start sound. Every lesson begins from here.

Step 5 · Save it

  1. Look at the display. Make sure the slot starts with 2, 3 or 4.
  2. Hold SHIFT. Press pad 16 (WRITE). The display shows Ptn (pattern).
  3. Press pad 2 (ENTER).
  4. Wait for donE (done). Your slot is saved.
How to know you have unsaved changes

A small dot appears at the right of the display. When you turn the S-1 off, unsaved changes are gone. The slot goes back to the last save.

Undo: put a slot back the way it was

Hold SHIFT. Press pad 15 (MENU). Turn TEMPO/VALUE to rL.Sd (Reload Sound). Press pad 2 (ENTER). The sound goes back to the last save. The notes stay. rLod (reload sound and notes) puts back the sound and the notes.

Copy a slot to another slot

Open the slot you want to copy. Hold SHIFT, press pad 15 (MENU). Turn to COPy (copy). Press ENTER. Turn TEMPO/VALUE to pick where it goes, like 3.01 (bank 3, slot 1). Press ENTER. Use this to move a lesson sound you like into bank 3.

Back up everything to a computer

Plug the S-1 into the Mac with the USB cable. Hold the PLAY button while you turn the power on. Wait 1 to 2 minutes. A drive called S-1 appears on the Mac. Copy the whole BACKUP folder somewhere safe. Eject the drive. Turn the S-1 off. Do this before a factory reset, and once a month.

Optional: record what you play

The S-1 can record the notes you play. It keeps them in the slot with the sound. This is called a riff. You do not need this for the lessons. It is fun, and it helps you hear a sound many times without playing it again.

Step 1 · Pick an empty slot

  1. Press PLAY if the S-1 is playing. It stops.
  2. Hold PATTERN. Press pad 4. Let go of PATTERN. This picks bank 4, the scratch bank.
  3. Press pad 1. The display shows 4.01 (bank 4, slot 1).
  4. Set the sound you want to record. The clean start is fine.

Step 2 · Pick a speed and a length

  1. Make sure PATTERN and STEP are not lit. If one is lit, press it.
  2. Turn TEMPO/VALUE. The number is beats per minute. 100 is a good start.
  3. Hold SHIFT. Press pad 4 (LAST). Let go of SHIFT.
  4. Turn TEMPO/VALUE to 16. That is one bar. 32 is two bars. 64 is four bars.
  5. Press pad 1 (EXIT).

Step 3 · Turn on the count-in

A count-in is four clicks before the recording starts. It gives you time to get ready.

  1. Hold SHIFT. Press pad 15 (MENU). Let go of SHIFT.
  2. Turn TEMPO/VALUE until the display shows Cnt.I (count-in). Press pad 2 (ENTER).
  3. Turn TEMPO/VALUE to 4.
  4. Press pad 1 (EXIT) until you are out of the menu.

Step 4 · Turn on the click

The click is a steady beat. It helps you play in time.

  1. Hold SHIFT. Press pad 15 (MENU). Let go of SHIFT.
  2. Turn TEMPO/VALUE to Ntro (metronome). Press pad 2 (ENTER).
  3. Turn TEMPO/VALUE to rEC (rec: click only while recording). The click plays only while you record.
  4. Press pad 1 (EXIT) until you are out of the menu.
Click too quiet?

Open the menu again. Turn to Ntr.L (metronome level). Press ENTER. Turn the number up. Press EXIT.

Step 5 · Record

  1. Press REC. It lights up. The S-1 is ready.
  2. Press PLAY. You hear four clicks.
  3. Play your riff on the Xkey or the pads. Start on the beat after the fourth click.
  4. The display shows rEc (recording). The loop goes round and round. Each time, it keeps what you play.
  5. When you are done, press REC. The light goes off. Recording stops. The loop keeps playing.

Step 6 · Listen and save

  1. Listen to the loop. Press PLAY to stop it. Press PLAY again to hear it again.
  2. Hold SHIFT. Press pad 16 (WRITE). The display shows Ptn (pattern).
  3. Press pad 2 (ENTER). Wait for donE (done). Your riff is saved.
If it sounds wrong

Wrong notes? Hold SHIFT. Press pad 15 (MENU). Turn to rL.Sq (reload sequence). Press ENTER. The notes are gone. The sound stays. Go back to Step 5. Too fast or too slow? Change TEMPO/VALUE and record again.

Check yourself
Save it

Save the clean start to slot 4-15. Now you can always come back to it.

01 · The map: a sound's journey across the panel

GOAL

You can point to the five sections on the panel and say what each one does, in one short sentence.

Remember

A slot holds a sound and its notes. Bank 1 is Roland's. Banks 2, 3 and 4 are yours. Save with SHIFT + 16 → ENTER.

Set your S-1 like this

Open slot 4-15, your saved clean start. Press SHIFT + HOLD. If you did not save it, do Lesson 00 steps 1 to 3 again in slot 4-15.

A sound is made in steps. Each step is a section on the panel. The sound goes from left to right across the top row.

Tap any knob, button or pad to see what it does. Tap again, or tap anywhere else, to close the note.
The S-1 top panel. Top row: jacks and volume. Left: the display and tempo knob. Middle: the five sound sections. Bottom: SHIFT, the small function pads, and the 13 keyboard pads.

The five sections printed on the panel

SectionIn plain wordsJob
OSCILLATORthe sound makerMakes the raw buzz. Picks the octave.
FILTERthe bright-or-dark knobTakes the fizz out of the buzz. Or leaves it in.
ENV (envelope)the note shaperDecides how a note starts, holds and ends. It does this once for every note.
LFOthe wobble makerRocks a setting back and forth, over and over, by itself.
EFX (effects)the decorationAdds echo and room sound at the very end.

The hidden sixth part: AMP

AMP is short for amplifier. It is the volume gate. It opens when you press a pad and closes when you let go. The S-1 has no AMP section with knobs. It is one hidden setting. Hold SHIFT and press the AMP pad to see it. Env means the envelope controls the volume. GAtE means plain on and off. We keep it on Env.

Solid arrows carry the sound: OSCILLATOR → FILTER → AMP → EFX. The two boxes at the bottom, ENV and LFO, make no sound. They reach up and move the other boxes for you.
The SHIFT rule

Many S-1 settings have no knob. To reach them, hold SHIFT and press a pad. Or hold SHIFT and turn a knob. The small words above each pad tell you its SHIFT job. The display shows the value. Turn TEMPO/VALUE to change it. Press pad 1 (EXIT) to leave.

Try it
  1. Hold a keyboard pad. Turn FREQ in the FILTER section down and up. The sound goes dark, then bright. That is the filter.
  2. Put FREQ back to MAX. Turn RELEASE in the ENV section to 12 o'clock. Press and let go of a pad. The note hangs on. That is the envelope.
  3. Put RELEASE back to MIN. Turn REVERB in the EFX section to 12 o'clock. Play. You are in a big room. That is an effect.
  4. Put REVERB back to MIN.

Save it

Nothing changed. You put every knob back. No save needed. The next lesson still starts from the clean start.

02 · What sound actually is

GOAL

You can name the three things about a sound wave that change what you hear.

Remember

Five sections. The sound goes OSCILLATOR → FILTER → AMP → EFX. ENV and LFO are helpers.

Set your S-1 like this

Open slot 4-15, the clean start. Press SHIFT + HOLD.

Sound is air wiggling back and forth. Fast wiggles sound high. Big wiggles sound loud.

Drag the sliders. More waves squeezed in means a higher note. Taller waves mean a louder note.

A speaker pushes air back and forth. Your ear feels those pushes. There is a third thing too: the shape of the wiggle. Shape is why a flute and a trumpet sound different on the same note. The word for shape-of-sound is timbre. Say it "TAM-ber". You can also just say "the colour of the sound".

Try it
  1. Press a keyboard pad. Listen.
  2. Hold SHIFT and press OCT+. Press the same pad. Same shape, faster wiggle, higher note.
  3. Hold SHIFT and press OCT− to go back.
  4. Turn the VOLUME knob at the top right down and up. Same shape, same speed, bigger wiggle.
Check yourself
Save it

Nothing to save. Set the octave back to 0: hold SHIFT and press OCT− and OCT+ at the same time.

The S-1 has four sound makers. Each green knob is the volume of one of them. You mix them like paint.

The square wave has a secret. Its top and bottom can be the same width, or different widths. A thin top sounds nasal and reedy. Hold SHIFT and press PWM DEPTH to set the width, 0 to 255. Hold SHIFT and press PWM SRC to pick what moves the width. NAn means you set it and it stays. LFO (LFO wave) makes it shimmer. Env (envelope) moves it once per note. PWM is short for "pulse width modulation". That just means "changing the width".

Two more knobs live in this section. RANGE picks the octave. 64′ is very low. 2′ is very high. 8′ is the middle. The white LFO knob lets the wobble bend the pitch. That is Lesson 09. Leave it at MIN for now.

Try it
  1. Hold a pad. You hear the saw alone. Buzzy and bright.
  2. Turn saw to MIN. Turn square to MAX. Hollow and woody. Same note, different colour.
  3. Turn square to MIN. Turn saw back to MAX.
  4. Now add SUB (Fader 2) up to 12 o'clock. Feel the floor shake. Hold SHIFT and press SUB OCT to pick -1oc (one octave down) or -2oc (two octaves down). Pick -1oc.
  5. Add a little NOISE (Fader 3), to 9 o'clock. Hear the hiss. Turn it back to MIN.
  6. Turn RANGE to 16′. Deeper. Turn it to 4′. Higher. Put it back to 8′.
If it sounds wrong
  • No sound at all: all four green knobs are at MIN, or FREQ is at MIN. Turn saw up and FREQ to MAX.
  • Knobs do nothing: you forgot SHIFT + HOLD after changing slot.
  • Too quiet and muddy: RANGE is at 64′ or 32′. Set it to 8′.
Check yourself
Save it

Leave the knobs like this: saw MAX, SUB 12 o'clock (SUB OCT = -1oc), square and NOISE MIN, RANGE 8′. Everything else as the table at the top. Save to slot 2-03: SHIFT + 16 → ENTER → donE (done). This is your "saw plus sub" sound. Lesson 04 starts from it.

You can make a sound dark or bright with one knob, and you can guess the sound before you turn it.

The filter is a gate. It lets the low parts of the sound through. It blocks the high, fizzy parts.

Each bar is one part of a sawtooth sound. Low parts are on the left. High fizzy parts are on the right. The purple line is the filter. Anything under the line gets through. Leave RESO at 0 for now. It is the next lesson.

A sawtooth is not one sound. It is a stack of quiet tones piled up. The stack is called the harmonics. The S-1 filter is a low-pass filter. That means it lets the low harmonics pass through. The knob that moves the gate is called FREQ. Turn it down and the high harmonics go away. The sound gets dark, like music through a wall. Turn it all the way down and there is no sound at all.

Try it
  1. Hold a low pad.
  2. Slowly turn FREQ (Knob 1) from MAX down to MIN. Bright, warm, dark, gone.
  3. Slowly turn it back up. That move is called a filter sweep. It is the most famous sound in techno.
  4. Stop at 12 o'clock. Play a few notes. Warm, not fizzy.
If it sounds wrong
  • No sound: FREQ is at MIN. Turn it up.
  • Nothing changes when I turn FREQ: forgot SHIFT + HOLD. Press it.
Check yourself
Save it

Leave FREQ at 12 o'clock. Everything else as the table. Save to slot 2-04: SHIFT + 16 → ENTER → donE (done).

You can make the filter "talk" with the RESO knob, and you know why it gets loud.

Resonance makes the edge of the filter louder. You hear a "wow" or a whistle right where the gate is.

Go back to the picture in Lesson 04. Drag RESO up. See the bump at the edge of the purple line? That bump turns up the harmonics right at the gate. Push RESO all the way up and the filter sings by itself. That is called self-oscillation. It means the filter has become its own sound maker.

Try it
  1. Hold a pad. Turn RESO (Knob 2) up to 3 o'clock. The sound gets thinner and sharper.
  2. Now slowly sweep FREQ (Knob 1) down and up. Hear it talk: "yeeoww". That is the squelchy acid sound.
  3. Turn RESO to MAX. Turn FREQ to about 10 o'clock. Let go of the pad. It still whistles. That is self-oscillation.
  4. Turn RESO back to 2 o'clock.
If it sounds wrong
  • It is too loud and shrill: RESO is high and FREQ is high. Turn FREQ down to 10 o'clock.
  • I hear no change: FREQ is at MAX. The gate is above all the harmonics, so the bump has nothing to grab. Turn FREQ to 12 o'clock.
Check yourself
Save it

Leave FREQ at 10 o'clock, RESO at 2 o'clock. Everything else as the table. Save to slot 2-05: SHIFT + 16 → ENTER → donE (done).

The robot hand can turn two knobs at once. It already turns the volume. Now we let it turn FREQ too.

Purple dashed line: where FREQ sets the gate. Green hill: the envelope pushing the gate up for a moment, then letting it fall.

This is the most important trick in the course. The orange ENV knob in the FILTER section (Knob 3) connects the envelope to the filter. Turn it up. Now every note starts bright and goes dark, "bow-w". The DECAY knob sets how fast it goes dark.

Try it
  1. Turn FREQ down to 9 o'clock. Play. Dull and dark.
  2. Turn FILTER ENV (Knob 3) up to 3 o'clock. Play short notes. Each one bites, "bow-w". That plucky bass is the heartbeat of techno.
  3. Slowly turn DECAY (Knob 6) up and down. The bite gets longer and shorter.
  4. Turn RESO up to 3 o'clock. Now it squelches too.
  5. Put RESO back to 2 o'clock. Put DECAY back to 10 o'clock.
If it sounds wrong
  • No bite: FILTER ENV is at MIN, or FREQ is already at MAX so there is nowhere to go. Set FREQ to 9 o'clock.
  • Bite is there but the note hangs on: SUSTAIN is up. Set it to MIN.
Check yourself
Save it

Leave FREQ 9 o'clock, RESO 2 o'clock, FILTER ENV 3 o'clock, DECAY 10 o'clock. Everything else as the table. Save to slot 2-07. This is your pluck bass. You will use it again in Lessons 10 and 11.

High notes have high harmonics. A fixed filter cuts them more. Keyboard follow lifts the filter as you play higher, so every note is equally bright.

Orange dots are notes. Purple is the filter. Left: the high note sits above the filter and gets cut, so it sounds dull. Right: the filter climbs with the notes.

The LFO is a slow wave that rocks a knob back and forth, forever. You pick what it rocks, how fast, and how much.

LFO is short for "low frequency oscillator". That means "a very slow wave". Too slow to hear as a note. Just right for rocking a knob. The envelope moves once per note. The LFO never stops. It rocks like a swing.

The LFO section has two knobs. RATE is the speed (Fader 1). WAVE FORM is the shape of the rocking: ramp up, ramp down, triangle, square, random (RND), or noise. The "how much" knobs live on the targets. The white LFO knob in OSCILLATOR rocks the pitch. The white LFO knob in FILTER rocks the brightness (Knob 4).

Target knobWhat you hearName for it
OSCILLATOR · LFOthe note bends up and downvibrato
FILTER · LFO (Knob 4)"wah-wah" or "wub-wub"filter wobble
SHIFT + PWM SRC = LFOthe square wave shimmersPWM
Try it
  1. Hold a pad for 10 seconds. A plain slow pad. No wobble yet.
  2. Turn FILTER LFO (Knob 4) up to 10 o'clock. Hold a pad. It breathes, wah-wah. That is the filter wobble.
  3. Turn RATE (Fader 1) up to 2 o'clock. Faster wobble. Back to 9 o'clock.
  4. Turn FILTER LFO back to MIN. Turn OSC LFO (the white knob in OSCILLATOR) up to 9 o'clock. Hold a pad. The pitch sways. That is vibrato.
  5. Turn WAVE FORM to RND. Random bubbles. Turn it back to triangle.
  6. Turn OSC LFO back to MIN. Turn FILTER LFO to 9 o'clock.
Two studio tips

Set the menu item LFO.S (LFO Sync) to On. Then RATE picks note lengths instead of a speed, and the wobble locks to the tempo.

In techno a tiny wobble is almost always better than a big one.

If it sounds wrong
  • No wobble: both LFO target knobs are at MIN. Turn one up.
  • Wobble is too fast to hear as a wobble: RATE is high. Turn Fader 1 down.
  • Pitch wobble sounds seasick: OSC LFO is too high. 9 o'clock is plenty.

These come last. They decorate a sound you already built. Build the sound first. Decorate after.

Effects (EFX)

  • DELAY (Fader 4) is an echo. The knob is how loud the echo is. Hold SHIFT and turn it to set the time between echoes. SHIFT + pad 13 opens the full delay menu.
  • REVERB is the sound of a room. The knob is how much room. Hold SHIFT and turn it to set how long the room rings. SHIFT + pad 14 picks the kind of room.
  • Chorus has no knob. It is the Cho (chorus) item in the menu. It copies the sound and smears the copy a little, so it feels wide.

Voices (POLY)

Hold SHIFT and press the POLY pad. Turn TEMPO/VALUE. Nono is mono: one note at a time. Uni is unison: all four voices stacked on one note, very fat. PoLy (poly, many notes) plays up to four notes at once. Chd (chord) plays a whole chord from one pad.

Arpeggiator (ARPEGGIO)

An arpeggio is a chord played one note at a time, up or down, in rhythm. The arpeggiator does it for you. Hold a chord and it runs. SHIFT + pad 8 turns it on. SHIFT + pad 9 picks the direction. SHIFT + pad 10 picks the speed. It needs POLY set to PoLy.

Try it
  1. Play some pluck notes. Dry.
  2. Turn DELAY (Fader 4) to 11 o'clock. Each note echoes. Turn REVERB to 10 o'clock. Now the echoes are in a room.
  3. SHIFT + POLY → PoLy (poly, many notes). Press pad 1 (EXIT). Hold three pads. A chord.
  4. SHIFT + pad 8 → On (on). Hold the same three pads. They run as a pattern. Press PLAY if nothing moves, so the clock runs.
  5. SHIFT + pad 10, turn to 1_16 (sixteenth notes). Faster.
  6. While it runs, slowly turn FREQ. That is a techno build.
If it sounds wrong
  • Arp does not run: POLY is still Nono, or the sequencer is stopped. Set PoLy, press PLAY.
  • Chord sounds like one note: POLY is Nono. Set PoLy.
  • Everything is a wash of echo: DELAY and REVERB are too high. Decoration should be small.

Save it

12 · Four-beat riff in pattern 14: borrow a sound, write four notes, save

GOAL

You can copy a sound you like into a spare slot, clear only its notes, step in four notes from the MiniLab, fix a wrong note, loop it, and save it.

Remember

A slot holds a sound and its notes. COPy moves a slot. CLEAR NOTE wipes the notes only. init wipes both. Save with SHIFT + 16 → ENTER.

Set your S-1 like this

Pick the bank you keep your sounds in (bank 3 in this course). Open the slot with the sound you want, here slot 16. Press PLAY if it is playing, so it stops. MiniLab on its "Roland S1 v2" program.

A riff is four notes that loop. You do not need to play in time. You put each note on a step by hand.

StepButtonsWhat happens
1 · Copy slot 16 to 14Open slot 16 · SHIFT + pad 15 (MENU) · turn to COPy (copy) · pad 2 (ENTER) · turn to 14 · pad 2 (ENTER)Slot 14 now has the same sound as 16.
2 · Open 14 and save a safety copyHold PATTERN, press the bank pad, let go, press pad 14 · SHIFT + pad 16 (WRITE) · Ptn (pattern) · pad 2 (ENTER)Wait for donE (done). If anything goes wrong later, rL.Sd brings this back.
3 · Clear the notes onlySHIFT + pad 11 (CLEAR NOTE) · pad 2 (ENTER)The notes are gone. The sound stays. Never use init (initialize) here. It wipes the sound too.
4 · Set one barSHIFT + pad 4 (LAST) · turn to 16 · pad 1 (EXIT)16 steps is one bar of four beats. The beats are steps 1, 5, 9 and 13.
5 · Enter the four notesREC dark · STEP lit · hold pad 1, play a key on the MiniLab · repeat on pads 5, 9, 13Each pad lights when it holds a note. Press a lit pad again to delete its note.

If the octave is wrong

The D one octave below C5 is D4. If the note sounds an octave off from what you want, pick D3 instead. The same rule works for any note: the number is the octave.

Try it
  1. Do steps 1 to 4. Slot 14 is empty and has your sound.
  2. Do step 5 with the keys D, D, A, A# on pads 1, 5, 9, 13.
  3. Press PLAY. Listen to the loop. Hold the MiniLab's keys over it.
  4. Hold pad 9. Turn TEMPO/VALUE one click up. The third note moves. Turn it back.
  5. Save (step 7).
If it sounds wrong
  • The sound changed when I cleared: you used init. Open MENU, turn to rL.Sd (reload sound), press ENTER.
  • Pressing a pad plays a note instead of setting one: STEP is not lit. Press STEP.
  • The loop is eight steps or runs long: LAST is not 16. Do step 4 again.
  • The riff vanished after power-off: it was not saved. Unsaved slots go back to the last save.
Check yourself
Save it
